## H1: Turkey teeth gone wrong: what actually fails

"Turkey teeth" became a media story because of a specific, real and avoidable pattern:
healthy teeth reduced to pegs for crowns, in patients who wanted a cosmetic change. The
coverage was often unfair to Turkish dentistry as a whole and entirely fair about that
pattern. Both things are true.

Here is what we see, ranked by how often it arrives at our clinic for revision, and what
can actually be done about each.

## H2: 1. Over-preparation — healthy teeth crowned unnecessarily

**What happened:** enamel removed from sound teeth to fit crowns, usually 8–20 units,
for an aesthetic complaint. Often the teeth were also devitalised (root-treated) to make
them easier to prepare.

**Can it be fixed?** The crowns can be replaced with better ones. The tooth structure
cannot be replaced, ever. This is why this failure is the serious one: the patient is
committed to crowns for life, with a replacement cycle every 10–15 years, and each cycle
removes a little more.

**What we can do:** replace poorly fitting crowns, treat the gum inflammation caused by
bad margins, and manage the teeth long-term. We cannot undo it, and anyone who says they
can is lying.

**Prevention** is the whole point of
[crowns vs veneers vs neither](/en/before-you-book/crowns-vs-veneers-what-you-actually-need/).

## H2: 2. Marginal failure and gum inflammation

**What happened:** crown margins that do not fit the tooth, or sit too far under the gum.
Result: persistent bleeding, bad odour, a dark line, and decay under the crown.

**Can it be fixed?** Usually yes. The crowns are remade with correct margins, after any
decay is treated. Outcome is generally good if the underlying tooth is still sound.

## H2: 3. Untreated disease under new restorations

**What happened:** crowns or implants placed over untreated periodontal disease or an
undiagnosed apical infection. Looks perfect on discharge day, fails at 6–24 months.

**Can it be fixed?** Yes, but in the right order: treat the disease first, stabilise, then
re-restore. It takes months, not a week — which is precisely why it was skipped the first
time. Patients often find this the hardest part to accept.

## H2: 4. Implant failure or malposition

**What happened:** implants that did not integrate, were placed in insufficient bone, or
were angled so that a restoration could only be made by compromising it.

**Can it be fixed?** Failed implants are removed; the site usually needs grafting and
4–6 months of healing before a new implant. Malpositioned but integrated implants are
sometimes restorable, sometimes must be removed. This is the most expensive category to
correct.

**Critical practical point:** bring the implant passport. If the system is unknown,
replacement components cannot be ordered and otherwise repairable cases have to be
completely redone.

## H2: 5. Bite and joint problems

**What happened:** the full-mouth restoration changed the vertical dimension or the way
the teeth meet. Symptoms: jaw pain, headaches, teeth that touch unevenly, ceramic that
keeps chipping.

**Can it be fixed?** Often yes, with occlusal adjustment or a splint; sometimes it needs
the restorations remade. Diagnosis has to come first — chipping is a symptom, not the
disease.

## H2: 6. Aesthetic failure

**What happened:** teeth that are too white, too long, too uniform, or that do not match
the face. Technically sound, cosmetically wrong.

**Can it be fixed?** Yes — remake the ceramics with a proper trial (mock-up worn before
anything is finalised). This is the least serious category and the most distressing to
live with.

## H2: What revision realistically involves

- **Assessment first.** Radiographs, periodontal charting, vitality testing, occlusal analysis. Anyone quoting revision without these is repeating the original mistake.
- **Usually two visits.** Disease treatment and healing cannot be compressed.
- **It costs more than the original.** Removing existing work, treating what is underneath and rebuilding is more work than doing it properly once.
- **Sometimes the answer is to do nothing yet.** Crowns that are functional but aesthetically imperfect on recently devitalised teeth are often better monitored than immediately replaced.

We will tell you which of these applies to you before you travel — send records to the
[second opinion](/en/before-you-book/dental-treatment-abroad-second-opinion/) page.

## H2: Where you stand

If the original clinic is still operating, start there: the guarantee, if there is one, is
theirs to honour. Keep everything in writing. If they refuse, are unreachable, or you have
lost confidence in them, get an independent assessment before spending anything further.

### FAQ
**Can Turkey teeth be reversed?** Removed enamel cannot be restored. Crowns can be
replaced with better-fitting ones; the tooth underneath stays as it is.
**How much does it cost to fix?** It varies with what failed and what is underneath —
it is consistently more than the original treatment. An assessment gives a real figure;
a remote estimate does not.
**Should I go back to the same clinic?** If they will honour their guarantee and you
still have confidence in their diagnosis, yes. Get an independent opinion first.
**Is this common?** Bad outcomes are a minority of cases and are concentrated in a
recognisable type of clinic. [The red flags](/en/before-you-book/turkey-dentist-red-flags/)
describe that type.
